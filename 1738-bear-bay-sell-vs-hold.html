Five and a half years of ownership, and the sale would return slightly less than the purchase price. That is before accounting for inflation — in today's dollars, $350,000 from mid-2021 is worth roughly $420,000, so the real loss of purchasing power is closer to $70,000.

Why the tax bill is so large on such a small gain

Because most of it isn't tax on a gain. It's depreciation recapture.

Every year the property has been a rental, you have taken a depreciation deduction — roughly $9,973 a year against the building portion of your basis. Over 5½ years that is about $54,353. Those deductions reduced your taxable rental income each year, which was a benefit.

When you sell, the IRS takes that back. Depreciation recapture is taxed at up to 25%, and unlike capital gains there is no favorable lower rate for holding longer. It is owed whether or not the property appreciated.

The result is that a property can sell for almost exactly what you paid and still generate a meaningful tax bill. That is what is happening here — over half of the estimated tax has nothing to do with profit.

This is also why timing matters less than owners expect. Waiting does not reduce recapture; it grows it. But waiting does allow the market to recover, and right now the market is the problem.

Section Six

The condition problem, stated plainly

We understand the motivation here is maintenance cost, so we want to be direct about how that interacts with selling.

Your file shows the air conditioning system and the appliances are original to 2004. That is 22 years. Both are well past typical service life.

In this market that matters more than it used to, for two reasons.

  • Buyers are paying for updated homes. The top sales in your zip code — $475,000 to $535,000 — are almost all 2014 construction or newer. At 2004 with original systems, your home competes on price rather than condition.
  • Insurance underwriting has tightened. Buyers frequently cannot get affordable coverage on older systems and roofs, which means they cannot get a loan, which means they cannot buy at any price you would accept.

So selling to avoid maintenance expense tends to produce the opposite. The buyer's inspector finds the 2004 A/C, and you either replace it, credit it, or lose the buyer. The cost arrives anyway — just under time pressure, at the worst possible moment, and with a price reduction attached.

Replacing the A/C on your own schedule costs what an A/C costs. Replacing it under a contract deadline, while a listing ages, costs considerably more and buys you nothing.

Section Seven

What we recommend

Renew the lease and defer the sale

Your tenant is in place through November 19. Renewing keeps $2,000 a month coming in, avoids the $3,132 monthly bleed of a vacant listing, and keeps you out of the worst selling season of the year.

It also means you are not selling into a market where the county has just marked your value down, the comparable home on the next street has sat 160 days, and mortgage rates are at a one-year high.

Budget the A/C as a planned replacement, not an emergency

It is 22 years old. It is going to need replacing whether you sell or hold, and the only question is whether you choose the timing or a buyer's inspector chooses it for you.

Doing it on your terms costs less, removes the single biggest objection at sale, and protects you from an emergency replacement with a tenant in place.

Revisit the sale when the market gives you a reason

The things that would change this analysis are specific and observable: mortgage rates easing, the Huntcliff Park inventory clearing, and the county's assessed value turning back up. We watch all three and will tell you when they move.

Nothing about this recommendation is permanent. It is about not selling into the worst conditions we have seen in this neighborhood.

We recognize the maintenance costs are real and frustrating, and we are not dismissing them. But the arithmetic says selling now converts a frustration into a loss — and it does not actually remove the A/C from your responsibility, it just moves it into a negotiation you will not control.
